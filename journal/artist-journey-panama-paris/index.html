Artist Journey: From Panama to Paris

The Three Continents That Shaped My Art

Most artists grow up in one place. I grew up moving between them. Each city left its mark on my practice, my philosophy, and the way I see light itself.

Panama: Where It All Began

I was born in Panama City in 2002. Growing up there taught me something fundamental: constant light changes everything. The sun here isn't gentle—it's relentless, present, undeniable. Shadows are sharp. Colors are saturated. There's no apologizing for brightness.

My earliest memories of art are inseparable from this light. Drawing in the afternoon meant battling glare. Painting meant understanding how heat affects materials. The natural world here isn't soft—it's vivid, immediate, demanding attention.

Panama gave me my visual vocabulary. The boldness. The saturation. The refusal to dim colors to make them "acceptable."

But as I got older, I felt something missing. The artistic community was smaller. The opportunities to engage with contemporary abstract practice were limited. I needed to step into a larger conversation about what contemporary art could be.

That's when Madrid called.

Madrid: Finding My Voice

I moved to Madrid in 2020, right into the pandemic. The timing was terrible and perfect simultaneously.

Terrible because the city was locked down, galleries were closed, and the normal pathways for meeting artists and curators were impossible. Perfect because it forced me to develop my practice in isolation, to develop what felt truly mine rather than absorbing influences in real-time.

Madrid's light is different from Panama's. It's angular, particular, architectural. The city's art scene—even in lockdown—was more accessible to me than I'd expected. I began learning from the gallery ecosystem. I saw how artwork moves through the market. I understood what it meant to position work professionally.

During those months, I started creating the pieces that would become my Panamanian Collection, exhibited through Artisan & Co. I was exploring form and emotion in new ways, beginning to understand abstraction not just as aesthetic choice but as methodology—a way of reclaiming my own story and deciding how I'd tell it.

Madrid taught me that artistic practice exists in dialogue. It's not solitary creation—it's conversation with the market, with other artists, with the history of the medium itself. I learned to listen to that dialogue without losing my own voice.

I created my Madrid Collection during this period, including "Symphony," a piece that marked a turning point in my work. I was becoming less interested in representation and more fascinated by pure abstraction—how form could suggest emotion without depicting it.

Paris: The Light That Changed Everything

I moved to Paris in September 2023. I was 21, and I felt ready to be in a city where contemporary art isn't peripheral—it's central to the culture.

But I arrived just as the light began to disappear.

By November, Paris was dark. The gloom that settles over northern Europe in winter is a completely different phenomenon from the abundant light I'd known in Panama and the angular light of Madrid. This was absence. Scarcity. A radical shift in the quality and quantity of available illumination.

I could have experienced this as creative limitation. Instead, it became generative constraint.

When light became rare, I became obsessed with it. I started studying how light behaves at its boundaries. How it refracts when passing from one medium to another. How the transition itself—the threshold—contains all the information you need to understand transformation.

Paris forced me to slow down in a way neither Panama nor Madrid had required. In the gloom of winter, rushing doesn't make sense. You move carefully. You pay attention to what little light exists. You notice things invisible in abundance.

This is where the refraction collection was born.

What Each City Gave Me

Panama: Saturation, boldness, the uncompromising power of natural light

Madrid: Professionalization, dialogue with the art market, understanding of how work circulates

Paris: Constraint, meditation, the philosophy that ordinary phenomena contain extraordinary depth

The Refraction Collection: Synthesis of Three Places

The work I'm creating now in Paris couldn't have emerged anywhere else. But it also couldn't exist without Panama's visual intensity and Madrid's artistic rigor.

The refraction collection brings together all three: the saturated colors of Panama, the geometric precision learned in Madrid, and the meditative quality that only Paris's winter darkness could teach.

Each piece is a conversation between these three lights, three artistic contexts, three versions of myself at different points in this journey.

Still Moving, Still Learning

People sometimes ask if I'll stay in Paris, or if there's another city on the horizon. The honest answer is: I don't know. But I've learned that geographic movement isn't escape—it's research. Each place teaches you something about light, about human connection, about what's possible within specific contexts.

I moved not to run away but to step forward into larger conversations about contemporary art. Each city expanded what I thought was possible. Each location demanded different things from my practice.

That willingness to be displaced—to seek new contexts, new light, new artistic conversations—might be my most important artistic skill. Not technical virtuosity, but openness to transformation through geography.

The Panama in my work will always be there: that saturated, unapologetic color. The Madrid will remain: that geometric sophistication. And now Paris is becoming integrated: that meditative slowness, that obsession with light's behavior at thresholds.

I'm not the same artist I was three years ago. I'm not even the same artist I was when I arrived in Paris six months ago. Each movement changed me.

That's what I'm trying to capture in this work: the fact that we're all being refracted by our contexts, bent into new shapes by the places we inhabit, transformed by light—literal and metaphorical—we encounter along the way.
